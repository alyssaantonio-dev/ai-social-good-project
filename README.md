# ai-social-good-project
## The problem and who it affects
  Residents in San Jose are dealing with food insecurity. The primary issue is not shortage of food, but rather a lack of reliable and easily accessible information. Some factors contributing to food insecurity in the city are affordability, due to high cost of living, and accessibility. This deeply affects low-income residents in neighborhoods where grocery options are limited and cost of living is high to the point where they have to choose between utilities and food. Our system is also suitable for the elderly who also struggle with food access and guidance with applying for food assistance. Although food banks and assistance programs do exist, residents in the area struggle to find updated information about resources closest to them. Numerous individuals also fail to determine their eligibility for food assistance, such as CalFresh, and end up missing out on the helpful benefits.
  
  The key failure point in our system is that that data given by AI is outdated and inaccurate making it difficult for users to find the correct information. AI is unable to fully understand real-world accessibility factors, therefore the users aren't given transportation options and real-time availability.  

## The AI capability used and why it fits
The system that we are proposing is quite straightforward. The AI's capabilities used is extremely fitting for this issue, as it gives users the direct means to find quick and accessible food assistance options without giving filler information and unviable options that are irrelevant to them. We found that Lab 2 and Lab 3 are the best suited for our system and what we want out of it, especially in regards to our failure points. Lab 2 focuses on data extraction and text based assistance that can provide the user with an organized structure of food resource information based on their location, which is applicable to our problem because it gives immediate links to food assistance options without giving a mass amount of information that is not helpful to the user at that moment in time, along with the output including a numbered list and bullet points, making the readability easy to follow and straightforward.Lab 3 focuses on image recognition allowing the user to simply upload a screenshot or image of their current location to help identify a number of nearby food resources. This system can also be extremely useful for users that are older in age or those who may struggle with typing out all the details of their location and taking the time to research things on their own. These capabilities improve user accessibility, which is our failure point for this system. It makes the process of finding food assistance simple and efficient.

## The workflow — what goes in, what the AI does, what comes out, and who acts on it, including screenshots
For lab 2, the user inputs a message, for example, "I am low on food to feed my family tonight. I live in downtown San Jose" AI is then asked to find nearby food banks and pantries in downtown San Jose. AI generates a list of immediate food options in downtown San Jose along with the address, phone number, and hours of operation. It also generates other resources, such as free service that connects the user to food assistance services and tips on dealing with low food accessibility. The user is given all the information AI could provide to act on their issue with food insecurity.

## Oversight and Tradeoff — Where does human review sit, and what does the one change cost?
Human review would ideally take place after the AI generates nearby food assistance recommendations, accessibility details, and transportation guidance. A volunteer, social worker, or community organization staff member could verify whether the listed food banks, soup kitchens, and assistance programs are currently open, accepting visitors, and accessible through public transportation. Human oversight is especially important because AI may provide outdated or even falsified information and can misunderstand the user’s exact location from an uploaded image. By having a human quickly review or confirm the AI-generated recommendations, the system will become increasingly more reliable and trustworthy for all users who need any type of food assistance. The tradeoff is that adding human review can reduce the speed and progress of the system, as one of the main advantages of AI is instantaneous responses. Verifying information manually takes extra time, staffing, labor, and resources. This could delay help for users who need immediate assistance and increase operational costs for organizations running the service. However, this tradeoff improves accuracy and reduces the risk of users being sent to unavailable or incorrect locations, which is crucial to the system benefitting anyone at all.
